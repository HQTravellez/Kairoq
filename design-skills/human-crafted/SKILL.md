# Human-crafted (not AI-looking)

Read together with the Frontend design skill: it explains how to plan a distinctive direction; this one lists the tells to remove and what to show instead.

People can tell a generated site in two seconds. They look for the same tells: purple-to-teal gradients, gradient text, glowing blobs, three or four identical icon cards, "Revolutionize your workflow", fake logos and testimonials, everything centred, every section built the same way. A designed site makes choices, shows the real product and says specific things.

## Tells to remove
- Gradient text, aurora/mesh gradients, blurred colour blobs, neon glows, glassmorphism everywhere.
- A row of 3-4 identical cards with an icon in a tinted square, a title and two vague lines. One such grid per site at most, and only when the content really is parallel.
- Every section centred, with the same eyebrow → heading → paragraph → cards pattern.
- Generic copy: "seamless", "revolutionize", "unlock", "empower", "all-in-one solution", "take it to the next level".
- Invented proof: fake customer logos, made-up testimonials, "Trusted by 10,000 teams", fake star ratings. If there is no proof yet, say what is true ("Founding cohort open", "Built in Toronto").
- Stock 3D blobs, abstract illustrations of floating shapes, emoji used as icons.

## What people do instead
- **One brand colour, used with restraint.** A background chosen for the subject (not the default cream or near-black), clear dark text, and ONE saturated brand colour for actions and the few things that matter. No second accent unless the brand has one.
- **Typography carries the brand.** A characterful display face (a heavy geometric grotesk like Bricolage Grotesque, a tight grotesk like Inter Tight at 800, or an editorial serif like Fraunces), very large (text-6xl to text-8xl), tight leading (leading-[0.95]) and tight tracking. If the brand has a hand-made detail (a drawn underline, a signature mark), use it once; do not invent one per section.
- **Show the real product, not icons.** Every feature is shown as a small, realistic slice of the product: a flight row "AC 852 · YYZ → LHR · Tue 08:15 · $742 · In policy", a policy check, a receipt, a notification. Real-looking names, places, times and amounts (clearly sample data), tabular numbers.
- **Specific, opinionated copy.** Short, concrete, a bit of personality: "Six tabs. Or one trip.", "From a sentence to a trip your CFO can justify." Say who it is for and what changes for them. Use contrast (before/after, them/us).
- **Every section has its own layout.** Mix: a left-aligned hero with a product shot bleeding off the edge; a before/after comparison; a sticky scroll story; a single big number; a quote from the founder; a dense feature table; a timeline. Alternate alignment and density.
- **Craft in the details.** Precise spacing on an 8 px rhythm, borders and radii that follow hierarchy (not one radius on everything), optical alignment, real focus states, a custom favicon, a thoughtful 404, an announcement bar only when there is a real offer. Labels and eyebrows only where they carry information.
- **Motion with intent.** Elements arrive once as you scroll (see the Framer-style motion skill); interactive demos react to the user; nothing loops forever except a subtle live indicator.
- **Imagery.** Real photography of people and places (with a consistent grade) or no photos at all. Device mockups showing the actual UI. Never AI-art backgrounds.

## Before you finish
Ask: could this site belong to any other company if the logo changed? If yes, add specifics: names, numbers, places, the founder's voice, a real offer.
