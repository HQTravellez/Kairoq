# Staff onboarding

New staff in a hotel, restaurant, shop or studio need to feel welcome and know exactly what to do in their first days. A good onboarding portal replaces a stack of paper and a dozen questions with one friendly page that tracks progress.

## Purpose
- A portal per new team member, built from data: name, role, start date, location, manager, buddy, schedule items, checklist tasks (with category, due day, owner, done state), training modules, documents to read or sign, and useful contacts.
- The new person ticks tasks off; the manager sees progress across all new starters in an admin table.

## Layout
- Welcome hero: "Welcome, {firstName}" in large friendly type, role and team, start date and a countdown or "Day 3 of your first week", a short note from the owner with portrait and signature.
- Progress summary: a ring or bar showing completed tasks out of total, plus "Next up" with the next 2-3 tasks.
- First week timeline: five days as a horizontal stepper on desktop and vertical on phones; each day lists time, activity, place and person (for example "09:00 Kitchen tour with Ana").
- People cards: manager, buddy and key contacts with photo, role, how to reach them and when.
- Checklist: grouped by category (Paperwork, Uniform and equipment, Systems access, Safety, Training) using Checkbox rows with a due label; completed items fade and move to the bottom of their group. Save state to the database.
- Learning track: modules as cards with duration, format (video, reading, shadowing) and status (Not started, In progress, Done).
- Documents: handbook, menu or service standards, safety procedures, with "Read" and "Acknowledge" actions.
- Outcomes: "You are fully set up when..." a short list of observable results for the end of week one.
- Practical info: address, parking, staff entrance, uniform, Wi-Fi name (not passwords), break rules.

## Palette and type
- Warm and welcoming but professional: off-white base, brand colour for progress, soft tinted cards for categories. Friendly rounded sans (Nunito Sans, DM Sans, Figtree).
- Avoid corporate grey; use photos of the real team and place.

## Details
- Celebrate milestones with a small confetti or check animation when a category is done (respect reduced motion).
- Mobile-first: most staff will open it on a phone; large tap targets and sticky progress at the top.
- Manager view: table of new starters with start date, progress bar, overdue tasks count and a reminder action.
- Never store or display passwords or sensitive personal documents in plain lists; link to secure upload forms.

## Avoid
- Long policy texts on the first screen, jargon, dense tables for the newcomer, checklists that do not save, and generic stock photos.
